# Captions for the standalone TVC figures

These captions belong **outside** the graphics. The figures intentionally have no overall titles, slide footers, or point-specific methodological notes. Full evidence and source links are in [TVC_Focused_Plot_Guide.md](C:/Users/tae06/CODE/tvc-testbed/research_outputs/professor/TVC_Focused_Plot_Guide.md).

**Figure 1. Mass versus thrust reference.** The five published close coaxial thrust-vectoring vehicles (black) are shown alongside our vehicle's current design estimate (blue). Dashed lines show constant ratios of plotted thrust to weight, \(T/(mg)=1\) and 2. The thrust references are heterogeneous: they include stated capacities, a fitted value, an approximate graphical reading, and a bench-derived model limit. They should not be interpreted as standardized measured maximum thrust or as comparative flight performance.

**Figure 2a. Close-system controller architecture versus demonstrated GNC function.** Five close two-axis coaxial TVC systems with flight evidence are shown in black, alongside our vehicle's current simulated fixed-target PID/cascade controller in blue. The blue point does not indicate free-flight evidence. Only the three controller categories occupied by this selected set are displayed; the two blank categories from the former combined chart are omitted, not ruled out globally. “Given trajectory” means a supplied tracking reference, not a common planning method. Empty cells describe this selected corpus; they do not establish global novelty.

**Figure 2b. Transferable-method controller architecture versus demonstrated GNC function.** This separate figure retains the same GNC y-axis as Figure 2a and extends the controller x-axis to five categories. It places three physically tested methods on other platforms: Li's servo-integrated NMPC tiltable quadrotor, Torrente's learned-dynamics MPC quadrotor, and Osedo's RL controller on a constrained one-axis TVC rig. These are methodological precedents, not close-system flight evidence. Wilson's simulation-only lander is not plotted.

**Figure 3. Validation setting versus demonstrated task.** Each published close-system point (black) represents the task and setting of the cited demonstration. Our vehicle (blue) denotes its current model/bench stage, not free flight. Chen's custom-controller point is the target-height ascent; the paper's separate hover experiment used a different control stack.

**Combined alternative — controller/policy architecture versus validation setting.** This figure combines the eight physically tested publications from the close-system and method-transfer control maps with their validation setting; it does not plot our vehicle or Wilson's simulation-only lander. The pink and blue translucent ellipses are **illustrative groupings** for close TVC systems and other platforms, respectively—not measured Ashby property envelopes; overlap does not change a paper's platform category. All dots are published papers. The vertical categories describe the cited demonstrations, not a method-quality score. Spannagl and Linsen report outdoor landing-related flights; Denton's outdoor task is launch/hover stabilization. Santos demonstrates indoor trajectory tracking, Chen a custom-controller target-height ascent, and Li and Torrente indoor quadrotor tracking. Osedo tests pitch control on a constrained one-axis TVC rig. The dot for Torrente denotes learned residual dynamics inside MPC, **not residual RL**. Different vehicle types share the plot only to show method transfer; empty cells are not field-wide novelty claims.

**Figure 4 — controller/policy architecture versus demonstrated task.** Eight papers with physical-system experiments are grouped by a representative task: attitude regulation, ascent/hover, trajectory tracking, or landing/diversion. Wilson is excluded because its descent plant was simulated, despite the real Jetson Nano processor; our vehicle is also not plotted. Osedo's real experiment was a constrained one-axis TVC attitude rig, not free flight. Spannagl and Linsen are the close TVC papers with outdoor landing-related demonstrations. The translucent close-TVC and other-platform ellipses are visual literature groupings, not quantitative envelopes or evidence that every interior combination has been tested. A paper may demonstrate more than the single task chosen for its dot.
